## 2. Exact differentiated group law

For affine points

\[
P_i=(x_i,y_i)
\]

and tangent vectors

\[
\dot P_i=(u_i,v_i),
\]

put

\[
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
\]

on the ordinary addition chart. Differentiating the slope gives

\[
\dot m=
\frac{(v_2-v_1)(x_2-x_1)-(y_2-y_1)(u_2-u_1)}{(x_2-x_1)^2}.
\]

For

\[
P_3=P_1+P_2
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
\dot x_3=2m\dot m-u_1-u_2,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\dot y_3=
\dot m(x_1-x_3)+m(u_1-\dot x_3)-v_1.
}
\]

The implementation also differentiates the tangent slope on the doubling chart. Every output is checked against an independently compiled `n`-torsion lift and against the linearized curve equation.

This gives an exact public addition compiler for both deformation directions. It does not require the scalar label of either point once the public endpoints and tangent states are supplied.

## 3. The charged module has rank one

At `j=0`, the `b`-direction is pure Weierstrass scaling:

\[
\dot x_b=\frac{x}{3b},
\qquad
\dot y_b=\frac{y}{2b}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
B(P)=
\frac{x(P)/y(P)}{x(G)/y(G)}.
}
\]

Thus `B` is not a new hidden state. It is an ordinary public endpoint coordinate ratio.

Because

\[
N=AB,
\]

and `B` is nonzero on the retained chart,

\[
\boxed{
A=\frac{N}{B}.
}

Therefore the two-coordinate charged tangent state has only one charge generator over the neutral field. Every Laurent-rational expression with odd endpoint charge can be written as

\[
\boxed{
B\,F(N,B^2,\text{other neutral data}).
}

The moduli tangent contributes new neutral information through `N`, `T`, and `R`, but it does not introduce a second independent generator-relative orientation bit.

## 4. Exact addition law for the surviving charge

Define

\[
e(P)=\frac{x(P)}{y(P)}.
\]

Since

\[
B(P)=e(P)/e(G),
\]

one has the exact cocycle law

\[
\boxed{
B(P+Q)=C_B(P,Q)B(P)B(Q),
}

where

\[
\boxed{
C_B(P,Q)=
\frac{e(G)e(P+Q)}{e(P)e(Q)}.
}

Every term is directly public. The identity is a coordinate tautology after the definition of `B`; it does not transport an additional hidden branch.

For the neutral product,

\[
N(P+Q)=C_N(P,Q)N(P)N(Q),
\]

and hence

\[
C_A=C_N/C_B.
\]

Again the `A` transfer is generated by the public coordinate charge and neutral data.

## 5. Exact doubling laws

Put

\[
T=x^3,
\qquad R=x\dot x_a,
\qquad D(T)=T^2+140T-392.
\]

For every nonexceptional retained point:

\[
\boxed{
T(2P)=
\frac{T(T-56)^3}{64(T+7)^3}.
}

The genuine moduli coordinate obeys

\[
\boxed{
R(2P)=
\frac{(T-56)\left(RD(T)-3T^2+42T\right)}{16(T+7)^3}.
}

The charged coordinate transfers are

\[
\boxed{
\frac{B(2P)}{B(P)}=
\frac{2(T-56)(T+7)}{D(T)},
}

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A(2P)}{A(P)}=
\frac{2\left(RD(T)-3T^2+42T\right)}{RD(T)}.
}

These are short rational evaluators. Their existence does not yield parity: both update an already known public charged state, and neither supplies the canonical integer path from `G` to `Q`.

## 6. Negation and GLV covariance

The exact transformations are

\[
A(-P)=-A(P),
\qquad
B(-P)=-B(P),
\]

while

\[
N(-P)=N(P),
\qquad
T(-P)=T(P),
\qquad
R(-P)=R(P),
\qquad
S(-P)=S(P).
\]

For the secp256k1 GLV map

\[
\phi(x,y)=(\beta x,y),
\qquad \beta^3=1,
\]

one has

\[
A(\phi P)=\beta^2A(P),
\qquad
B(\phi P)=\beta B(P),
\]

and `N,T,R,S` remain fixed.

Thus the entire endpoint charge of the moduli-tangent pair transforms exactly like the public coordinate ratio `x/y`.

## 7. Even and odd orbit factors

Let

\[
r=\frac{n-1}{2}
\]

and define, for `X=A` or `X=B`,

\[
P_{\rm even}(Z)=
\prod_{\substack{1\le k<n\\k\text{ even}}}
(Z-X([k]G)),
\]

\[
P_{\rm odd}(Z)=
\prod_{\substack{1\le k<n\\k\text{ odd}}}
(Z-X([k]G)).
\]

Because `n` is odd, the partner `n-k` has opposite parity, and

\[
X([n-k]G)=-X([k]G).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_{\rm odd}(Z)=(-1)^rP_{\rm even}(-Z).
}

Their product is a polynomial in `Z^2`. The public squared orbit identifies each unordered pair

\[
\{X,-X\},
\]

but does not select the even member.

On the sixteen-curve corpus the even factors are dense or nearly dense. Across both charged coordinates the largest number of zero coefficients is four. Their coefficient sequences attain the generic finite-window Berlekamp-Massey complexity. This is finite evidence against a short constant-coefficient recurrence, not an unrestricted circuit lower bound.

## 8. The secp256k1 multiplier graph

For the subgroup order `n`, exact arithmetic verifies

\[
\operatorname{ord}_n(2)=\frac{n-1}{64}.
\]

This order is odd. Moreover

\[
2^{\operatorname{ord}_n(2)/3}=\lambda^2\pmod n,
\]

so the GLV eigenvalue lies in the doubling subgroup.

After adjoining negation, the action generated by

\[
2,\lambda,-1
\]

has

\[
\boxed{32}
\]

orbits on the pair quotient. If only squared or neutral transfer data are supplied, one anchor fixes one orbit sign and leaves thirty-one independent cycle signs.

This count is not itself a lower bound against every algorithm. It specifies the remaining gauge that any open transport or cycle-label mechanism must remove.
