# UORC-056 H-RPCX compact specification gap V4 — audited interpretation

## Status

Elementary implicit-characterization lemma. This is **not** a proof of compression, a polynomial-time algorithm, or even evidence that such an algorithm exists.

## Exact statement

Let `f` be a function on an odd cyclic subgroup generated by `G`. The canonical parity word is the unique function satisfying

- `f(O) + f(-G) = 2`;
- `f(Q) + f(Q-G) = 0` for every `Q != O`.

Equivalently,

`(I + T) f = 2 delta_O`,

where `T` translates by `-G` and `delta_O` is one at the identity and zero elsewhere.

## Proof

For every non-identity point, the local equation says that the value must change sign when moving by one generator step. Starting with `f(O)=1`, this gives the alternating word

`+1,-1,+1,-1,...`.

Because the cycle has odd length, the final canonical index `n-1` is even. The wrap equation at `O` fixes the initial value to `+1`. Hence the unique solution is

`f([k]G)=(-1)^k`.

This proof is correct but elementary: it is essentially the definition of canonical parity written as a local system on an odd cycle.

## What it actually proves

It proves only that parity has a short **implicit description**. The equation can be stated without writing an exponential table and without placing the hidden scalar in the syntax of the equation.

This descriptive fact has no standalone algorithmic force. A short equation defining a function is not the same as a fast procedure evaluating that function at a randomly supplied encoded point.

## Why it does not advance H-PCX by itself

To evaluate the solution at `Q=[k]G` by direct propagation, one must know or traverse the generator distance from `O` to `Q`. That distance is exactly the hidden discrete logarithm.

The explicit inverse

`(I+T)^(-1)=(1/2)(I-T+T^2-...+T^(n-1))`

contains all `n` shifts and therefore gives no polylogarithmic random-access procedure.

Accordingly, V4 proves none of the following:

- existence of a classical polynomial-time parity algorithm;
- existence of a compact computable state;
- existence of a short arithmetic circuit;
- existence of a practical evaluator for secp256k1;
- any nontrivial upper bound on parity complexity.

## Correct research use

The equation may serve as a normalization or target identity for later work. A future candidate must add a genuine public random-access mechanism that evaluates the unique solution at an elliptic-curve point in `poly(log n)` total cost.

Without that additional mechanism, V4 is only an elementary characterization, not a positive algorithmic result.
