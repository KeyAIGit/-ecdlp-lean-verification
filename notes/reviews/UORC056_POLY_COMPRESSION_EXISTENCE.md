# UORC-056 Polynomial Compression Existence Hypothesis

## Status

Open breakthrough target with proved logical and representation-class boundaries.

No classical polynomial-time exact parity algorithm is claimed.

## Central question

Let `E` be a prime-order `j=0` elliptic curve over a prime field, let `G` generate a subgroup of odd prime order `n`, and let `Q=[k]G` with canonical scalar `0<=k<n`.

Does exact canonical parity admit a uniform classical evaluator whose complete time, memory, preprocessing, advice, representation, precision, and branch-selection costs are polynomial in `log n`?

## H-PCX

There exists a constant `c`, independent of the group size, and a uniform public construction which supplies:

1. a state `S(E,G,Q)` described by at most `(log n)^c` bits or field-sized components;
2. a public procedure constructing `S` in at most `(log n)^c` total cost;
3. a public decoder `D` returning exact canonical parity from `S` in at most `(log n)^c` total cost;
4. no hidden table, coefficient set, preprocessing, advice, extension degree, precision, memory, or branch-selection cost larger than `(log n)^c`.

## Exact logical strength

H-PCX is equivalent to a classical polynomial-time exact parity algorithm.

- H-PCX gives the algorithm by composing `S` and `D`.
- Any polynomial-time parity algorithm gives H-PCX by choosing `S(Q)=Q` and using the algorithm as `D`.

Exact parity is polynomial-time equivalent to ECDLP: a parity oracle reveals the least significant bit, after which that bit is removed and the point is divided by two; repeating recovers the complete scalar.

Therefore, for the declared uniform family:

`H-PCX  <=>  exact parity in P  <=>  ECDLP in P`.

A nonconstructive proof could precede an optimized implementation, but it would already prove classical polynomial-time solvability. H-PCX is not a substantially easier theorem hiding below the algorithm.

## Effectivity dichotomy

A representation-free existential compression claim is trivial: choose a two-state space and define the state to equal the unknown parity answer.

Once public polynomial-time construction and decoding are required, the compression is already an algorithm.

Meaningful intermediate theorems must therefore restrict the representation language: a declared module, recurrence grammar, circuit gate set, CM/Miller/theta state, rational-function class, or other falsifiable family.

## H-RPCX

The stronger recoverability hypothesis fixes an effectively enumerable public feature language and requires:

- every feature to have polynomial description and evaluation cost;
- exact parity to lie in a bounded span or algebra generated by polynomially many features;
- exact coefficients or structure to be recoverable from a finite full-rank known-scalar system;
- verification and all search costs to satisfy the complete cost gate.

H-RPCX implies H-PCX. It is useful only after the feature language is fixed and forbidden from containing the unknown parity function circularly.

## Proved boundaries

### 1. Full cyclic shift span

For every odd `n`, exact canonical parity and its cyclic translates span the complete `n`-dimensional function space.

Consequences:

- no proper low-dimensional linear translation-invariant module contains parity;
- no constant-coefficient cyclic linear recurrence of order below `n` generates parity;
- every additive Fourier character occurs with nonzero coefficient.

### 2. Autonomous phase-state lower bound

Any exact deterministic state updated by one position-independent rule under `k -> k+1` must distinguish all `n` scalar positions.

Consequences:

- autonomous two-, three-, and six-phase root transports cannot work;
- any autonomous phase automaton with fewer than `n` states is impossible.

### 3. Exact rational degree in one character coordinate

If scalar position is idealized as `z=omega^k`, the minimum degree of a rational function returning exact parity is `(n+1)/2`.

The optimal construction separates even and odd roots by two half-cycle products.

### 4. Short recurrence is insufficient

Those half-cycle products satisfy a first-order recurrence, yet generic random-access evaluation still costs approximately `sqrt(n)` arithmetic operations. A recurrence contributes to H-PCX only together with a polynomial-in-`log n` jump-ahead theorem.

## Closed proof strategies

- low-rank all-translation parity matrices;
- small linear modules containing parity and closed under `Q -> Q+G`;
- short constant-coefficient cyclic recurrences for parity;
- sparse additive-character decompositions;
- autonomous constant-phase coherent transport;
- low-degree one-character rational decoders.

## Remaining positive mechanisms

A successful construction must escape all proved boundaries. Remaining classes include:

- nonlinear coordinate-dependent states;
- high-degree functions with genuinely short straight-line programs;
- polylogarithmic modular-composition or jump-ahead identities;
- query-dependent CM, Miller, theta, elliptic-unit, or p-adic transport;
- a direct joint evaluator for the GLV carry and Kummer sector;
- another secp256k1-specific mechanism outside the generic-group model.

## Admission requirements for every new class

1. State the exact grammar or representation class.
2. Prove public noncircular construction from `E,G,Q`.
3. Prove exact decoding on every subgroup point.
4. Give a polynomial-in-`log n` random-access evaluation bound, not merely a local recurrence.
5. Charge all advice, coefficients, preprocessing, memory, extension, precision, and branch choices.
6. Verify on frozen and held-out toy curves.
7. Produce symbolic proof and Lean formalization for the reusable algebraic kernel.
8. Keep negative conclusions scoped to the declared class.

## Decision rule

Promote H-PCX only after the complete effective evaluator and full-cost theorem are proved. Promote H-RPCX only after the fixed feature language, exact recoverability, and bounded extraction process are also proved.
