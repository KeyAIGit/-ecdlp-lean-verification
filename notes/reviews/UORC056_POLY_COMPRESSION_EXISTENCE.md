# UORC-056 Polynomial Compression Existence Hypothesis

## Status

Open structural hypothesis. This is not a claim that a classical polynomial-time parity algorithm is known.

## Central question

Let E be a prime-order j=0 elliptic curve over a prime field, let G generate the prime-order subgroup of order n, and let Q=[k]G with canonical scalar 0<=k<n. The target is exact canonical parity.

The hypothesis asks whether the exact parity map factors through a uniformly constructible state whose description size, construction cost, memory, and decode cost are polynomial in log n.

## Hypothesis H-PCX

There exists a constant c and a uniform public construction which, for every curve in a specified secp256k1-type family, produces:

1. a state space V(E,G) of dimension at most (log n)^c;
2. a public state map S(E,G,Q) in V(E,G), computable in at most (log n)^c field/group operations and with at most (log n)^c bits/elements of advice or representation;
3. a public exact decoder D(E,G,S) returning (-1)^k for every Q=[k]G;
4. transition rules for all state operations used by S and D whose construction and evaluation are also polynomial in log n;
5. no hidden table, preprocessing, representation, precision, memory, or branch-selection cost larger than polynomial in log n.

Equivalently, the oriented-root parity functional factors through a polynomial-dimensional, uniformly constructible public state.

## Why this is a useful existence target

A proof of H-PCX would imply the existence of a classical polynomial-time parity algorithm, and therefore a classical polynomial-time ECDLP solver via deterministic bit peeling. The theorem can in principle be proved before an optimized concrete evaluator is written.

The value of H-PCX is that it separates two tasks:

- structural existence: prove that the enormous exact parity table compresses into a small stable state;
- extraction: recover an explicit basis, transitions, and decoder from that small state.

If the structural theorem additionally shows that V(E,G) is spanned by an effectively enumerable public feature family with polynomially bounded descriptions, then an explicit evaluator is recoverable in principle by exact linear algebra and bounded synthesis on known-scalar samples.

## Stronger recoverability form H-RPCX

There exists an effectively enumerable public feature family Phi={phi_i} such that:

- every phi_i has description and evaluation cost polynomial in log n;
- parity belongs to the span/algebra generated by at most (log n)^c features;
- the relevant coefficient field/extension has degree polynomial in log n;
- a finite set of known-scalar samples produces a full-rank exact identification system.

Under H-RPCX, once the structural bound is proved, an explicit representation is algorithmically recoverable by finite search over bounded feature descriptions plus exact linear algebra.

## What would count as a proof

A valid proof must establish all of the following.

### A. Compression bound

Prove dim V(E,G) <= poly(log n), or an equivalent rank/circuit/state-size bound.

### B. Public constructibility

Prove that V, S, and all transition data are generated from public E,G,Q without access to k and without exponential advice.

### C. Exact containment

Prove that the parity functional, equivalently the oriented-root ratio, lies in or factors through V for every nonzero subgroup point.

### D. Efficient dynamics

Prove that the operations needed to evaluate S(Q), including doubling, GLV/CM action, Miller/theta transport, modular composition, or any proposed recurrence, act on V with polynomial cost.

### E. Exact decoding

Prove that D(S(Q))=(-1)^k identically, not statistically.

### F. Full cost gate

Charge preprocessing, advice, memory, representation, precision, query, online computation, and branch extraction. Every term must be polynomial in log n.

## Candidate proof strategies

1. Low-rank theorem: construct a matrix/tensor of oriented-root evaluations and prove its rank is poly(log n).
2. Stable-module theorem: show that the algebra generated by public CM/GLV/Miller operations closes on a poly(log n)-dimensional module containing parity.
3. Short-recurrence theorem: prove that the oriented state obeys a recurrence of order poly(log n) with publicly constructible coefficients.
4. Representation-theoretic theorem: decompose the orientation problem into finitely many characters/weights and prove that only poly(log n) components survive.
5. Algebraic-compression theorem: prove that Y_G has exponential degree but polynomial straight-line or modular-composition complexity.
6. Probabilistic existence theorem: define a finite explicit distribution over public constructions and prove positive success probability together with exact verification; this proves existence but must still meet the uniform cost gate.

## Non-goals and invalid shortcuts

The following do not prove H-PCX:

- finite interpolation of Y_G;
- an exponential lookup table;
- nonuniform advice of size Omega(sqrt(n)) or larger;
- a representation whose coefficients already encode all parity values;
- high polynomial degree alone;
- toy-curve fitting without a uniform theorem;
- statistical correlation instead of exact parity;
- a generic-group construction, which would conflict with known generic lower bounds.

## Relation to the search engine

This hypothesis is suitable for theorem-search orchestration because it decomposes into falsifiable subgoals. The engine should alternate between:

- proposing candidate state spaces/modules/recurrences;
- exact toy instantiation and rank measurement;
- held-out transfer;
- symbolic identity discovery;
- CAS proof and independent replay;
- Lean formalization of algebraic kernels;
- explicit cost accounting;
- generation of counterexamples and scoped closure lemmas.

The engine should not treat failure to find a basis as evidence that H-PCX is false. A negative result is meaningful only when a declared class is exhaustively or theoremically closed.

## Logical strength

H-PCX is weaker than presenting a concrete optimized implementation, but it is not weak mathematics. Proving H-PCX would already establish that the exact parity function admits a uniform polynomial-time classical evaluation mechanism. H-RPCX goes further by guaranteeing that, after the existence theorem is known, a concrete representation can be recovered by a finite effective procedure.

## Decision rule

Promote H-PCX to theorem status only after A-F are proved. Promote H-RPCX only after effective enumerability and full-rank recoverability are also proved.
