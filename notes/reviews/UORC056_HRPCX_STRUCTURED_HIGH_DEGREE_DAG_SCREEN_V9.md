# UORC-056 H-RPCX structured high-degree macro-DAG screen V9

## Status

Deterministic discovery screen. No parity algorithm was found.

This is deliberately not reported as an exhaustive negative theorem. Only the first unary macro layer is unpruned. Deeper layers use a fixed deterministic beam, and the final exact binary join is complete only over the retained library.

## Purpose

V8 proves that a direct rational decoder from `x,y` needs at least 254 charged binary arithmetic DAG nodes. Blindly enumerating all programs at that size is infeasible.

V9 therefore searches structured families in which very high algebraic degree is generated by a small number of macros, while every macro is charged by its expanded arithmetic-DAG cost.

For example, the macro

`pow2_254(z)=z^(2^254)`

is represented by one search transition but is charged as 254 repeated squarings. The search does not treat enormous public exponents as free operations.

## Frozen corpus

The same symbolic construction is evaluated on all nonzero points of five deterministic prime-order `j=0` toy instances:

- `(p,n)=(43,31)`;
- `(67,79)`;
- `(79,67)`;
- `(127,127)`;
- `(163,139)`.

The joint target contains 438 exact parity values.

## Base states

The base library contains 22 public field-valued states:

- `x([s]Q)` and `y([s]Q)` for `s` in `1,2,3,4,5,7,8`;
- `x(Q) +/- x(G)`;
- `y(Q) +/- y(G)`;
- `x(Q)+y(Q)` and `x(Q)-y(Q)`;
- `x(Q)^3`;
- `beta*x(Q)` for the public cubic CM root `beta`.

The small scalar multiples are explicit public operations. They are used only as declared starting primitives in this screen; a production cost ledger must also charge their group-operation chains.

## Macro catalogue

The 43 unary macros include:

- public affine scalings and shifts;
- negation and safe inversion;
- repeated-squaring powers through `2^254`;
- repeated-cubing powers;
- quadratic, cubic and sextic residue exponents;
- public `n`-dependent powers;
- several Mobius transforms;
- the declared `j=0` Lattes map.

Every power macro stores a conservative expanded multiplication/squaring cost.

## Search procedure

1. Evaluate every base state on the complete joint corpus.
2. Apply all macros exactly and merge identical semantic vectors.
3. Keep all 787 distinct states at depth one.
4. At depths two through four, retain the best 4000 states under the fixed ordering:
   exact parity errors, then expanded cost, then semantic bytes.
5. Search exact final joins `A+B`, `A-B`, `A*B`, and `A/B` by complement lookup over the complete retained library.

The binary join test is exact relative to the retained states.

## Replay result

The deterministic counts are:

- depth 1: 787 new states;
- depth 2: 20,475 new states before pruning;
- depth 3: 95,690 new states before pruning;
- depth 4: 90,931 new states before pruning;
- retained joint semantics: 12,809.

No exact unary candidate and no exact binary join candidate was found.

The best retained state still disagreed with parity on 195 of the 438 points. It was

`chi2(gx*(x(Q)-y(Q)))`,

with an expanded toy-operation cost of 11. This near result is diagnostic only and is not promoted.

## Interpretation

The first structured high-degree screen did not expose an easy parity decoder. More importantly, the new search machinery now distinguishes:

- search macro depth;
- expanded arithmetic-DAG cost;
- exact semantic equality;
- heuristic beam pruning;
- exact joins inside the retained library.

Thus a 254-squaring operation can be investigated without being mislabeled as one arithmetic gate.

## What is not proved

V9 does not prove:

- that no high-degree low-DAG-size parity evaluator exists;
- completeness beyond the first macro layer;
- absence of a candidate discarded by the beam;
- absence of multiregister circuits;
- absence of shared binary subgraphs before the final join;
- absence of a secp256k1-specialized circuit;
- any arithmetic-circuit lower bound.

## Next target

The next implementation should replace unary chains by a true multiregister DAG template:

1. every gate may consume any two earlier registers;
2. shared intermediate nodes are charged once;
3. high-degree power macros remain expanded in the cost ledger;
4. synthesis uses counterexample-guided templates rather than raw enumeration;
5. candidates must transfer unchanged across frozen and held-out curves;
6. exact candidates proceed to symbolic identity discovery before any secp256k1 claim.
