# UNIFORM-ORIENTED-ROOT-CIRCUIT-056

## C17: odd symmetric GLV invariants and the residue-zero projection boundary

Date: 2026-08-13

Status: **the GLV trace is a second exact odd branch observable, distinct from the C16 cubic norm. In the cubic function-field extension it is exactly the residue-zero projection. More generally, every nonzero constant linear odd invariant `alpha*Trace(Z)+beta*Norm(Z)` has quotient-divisor support at least `(n-1)/6-4` and pole degree at least `(n-1)/12-2` on secp256k1. Thus neither trace, norm, nor any constant linear combination collapses to a bounded-support or sublinear-degree invariant. This is not an unrestricted arithmetic-circuit lower bound. A nonlinear rational combination of the invariant generators, or a short recurrence for their residue components, remains open.**

Only a publicly generated small `j=0` extension corpus and public secp256k1 constants are used. No external unknown scalar, wallet, private key, or production target is accepted.

## 1. Cubic fixed-field decomposition

Let

```text
E: y^2=x^3+7,
phi(x,y)=(beta*x,y),
beta^2+beta+1=0.
```

Put

```text
K=F_p(E),
L=K^<phi>=F_p(y),
t=x^3=y^2-7.
```

Every `C in K` has a unique decomposition

```text
C=C0+x*C1+x^2*C2,
C0,C1,C2 in L.                                   (C17.1)
```

For its three GLV conjugates, the elementary symmetric functions are

```text
boxed:
Trace(C)=3*C0,                                   (C17.2)

e2(C)=3*(C0^2-t*C1*C2),                         (C17.3)

Norm(C)=C0^3+t*C1^3+t^2*C2^3-3*t*C0*C1*C2.      (C17.4)
```

Thus trace is not merely another heuristic aggregate. It is the exact projection onto the `x`-exponent class zero modulo three.

## 2. Two odd invariant generators

For the C15 endpoint gauge, write

```text
Z_i(P)=Z(phi^i(P)), i=0,1,2.
```

Define

```text
E1=Z_0+Z_1+Z_2,
E2=Z_0 Z_1+Z_1 Z_2+Z_2 Z_0,
E3=Z_0 Z_1 Z_2.                                  (C17.5)
```

All three lie in `F_p(y)`. Under the unresolved global branch change

```text
Z -> -Z,
```

the transformation law is

```text
E1 -> -E1,
E2 ->  E2,
E3 -> -E3.                                      (C17.6)
```

C16 studied `E3`. C17 adds `E1=Trace(Z)` and the complete constant linear odd family

```text
F_(alpha,beta)=alpha*E1+beta*E3.                 (C17.7)
```

A public compact evaluator for any nonzero member of this family would select the missing global branch.

## 3. Nonexceptional orbit types

Away from at most four nonzero GLV orbits meeting the C15 correction support, the three local valuations of the conjugates are

```text
s_0,s_1,s_2 in {+1,-1}.                          (C17.8)
```

Let `N_r` denote the number of nonzero GLV orbits containing exactly `r` values `+1`.

For an orbit of type `r=2`, exactly one conjugate has a simple pole and the other two vanish. Therefore

```text
ord(E1)=-1,
ord(E3)=+1.                                      (C17.9)
```

If `alpha != 0`, the trace term is the unique leading pole, so `F_(alpha,beta)` has a pole there.

For an orbit of type `r=3`, all three conjugates vanish. Hence every nonzero `F_(alpha,beta)` with `alpha != 0` vanishes there.

If `alpha=0` and `beta != 0`, then

```text
ord(E3)=s_0+s_1+s_2 in {-3,-1,+1,+3},            (C17.10)
```

so every nonexceptional orbit lies in the divisor support.

Consequently, for every nonzero constant pair `(alpha,beta)`, the divisor support contains at least all nonexceptional orbits of types `r=2` and `r=3`.

## 4. Exact negation pairing

There are

```text
N=(n-1)/3                                         (C17.11)
```

nonzero order-three GLV orbits.

Negation sends

```text
{k,lambda*k,lambda^2*k}
```

to a distinct GLV orbit. The orbit cannot be fixed by negation because that would imply `-1` belongs to the order-three subgroup generated by `lambda`.

Since `n` is odd, negation flips the parity of every index. Therefore it sends type `r` to type `3-r`, and

```text
boxed:
N_0=N_3,
N_1=N_2.                                        (C17.12)
```

It follows exactly that

```text
boxed:
N_2+N_3=N/2=(n-1)/6.                            (C17.13)
```

At most four of these orbits can meet the endpoint correction. Hence

```text
boxed:
#supp div(F_(alpha,beta)) >= (n-1)/6-4.          (C17.14)
```

This bound is uniform over every nonzero constant pair `(alpha,beta)`.

## 6. Why the residue decomposition is not yet compression

Equation `(C17.2)` shows that trace extracts `C0` in one formal operation. Equation `(C17.4)` expresses norm through three components. But a constant-size identity does not make the components cheap.

A positive route must still give a public recurrence or transposed evaluator for

```text
C0(y(Q)), C1(y(Q)), C2(y(Q))                    (C17.16)
```

without constructing their linear-size rational representations, evaluating three independent endpoint gauges, or supplying the branch as advice.

Merely separating coefficients by exponent modulo three reduces a dense representation by a constant factor only.
