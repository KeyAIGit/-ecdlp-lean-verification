# HEISENBERG-PROJECTIVE-CROSS-RATIO-C055

Date: 2026-08-13

Status: toy-only exact projective-invariant study. No external point or production target is accepted.

For one C053 weight pair, let

```text
P0=Q,
P1=Q+G,
P2=Q+phi(G),
P3=Q+phi^2(G),
[ij]=det(v(P_i),v(P_j)).
```

Define the projective cross-ratio and its complement

```text
X=[01][23]/([02][13]),
Y=[03][12]/([02][13]).
```

The Plucker identity gives

```text
X+Y=1.
```

Both values descend exactly to `F_p`. They are invariant under independent rescaling of all four theta vectors and under one common change of the two-dimensional theta basis. Thus this object retains the full generic projective invariant of four characteristic-root theta lines, rather than compressing three lines to one Maslov bit.

The six anharmonic transforms of a cross-ratio reduce, at the quadratic-character level, to the two bits represented by `chi(X)` and `chi(1-X)`.

Eight frozen toy cases were screened using `X_ab`, `1-X_ab` for all three weight pairs, every affine pencil over `F_p`, and every multiplicative subset.

```text
affine formula instances: 1,885,184
exact target identities:  0
```

On the largest groups `n=3469,4021`, the best accuracies remain near random scale. The finite set where a Plucker coordinate vanishes is publicly recognizable; no exact identity appears on the regular locus.

Conclusion: the first complete fixed-rank projective invariant does not select the required orientation. For a fixed finite collection of projective lines, all rational PGL2 invariants are generated by cross-ratios, so another scalar invariant of the same rank is not a genuinely new mechanism.
