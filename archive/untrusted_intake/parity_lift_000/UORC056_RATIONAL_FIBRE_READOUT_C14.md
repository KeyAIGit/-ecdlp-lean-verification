## 2. Homogeneous rational readout theorem

Let `X` be an integral variety, `L` a line bundle on `X`, and

```text
T=L^x
```

its total space with the zero section removed. The group `G_m` acts by fibre scaling.

A rational function `R` on `T` has fibre weight `w` when

```text
R(lambda*v)=lambda^w R(v).                       (C14.4)
```

### Theorem

For every integer `w`, rational weight-`w` functions on `T` are naturally identified with nonzero rational sections of

```text
L^(-w).                                          (C14.5)
```

In particular:

```text
weight 0: R is a rational function on the base X;
weight 1: R is exactly a rational section of L^(-1), hence a rational fibre coordinate;
weight -1: R is exactly a rational section of L.
```

### Proof

At the generic point of `X`, the fibre of `L` is a one-dimensional vector space over `K(X)`. A homogeneous rational function of weight `w` is a homogeneous coordinate of degree `w` on this one-dimensional space. Under a change of local frame, its coefficient transforms by the transition law of `L^(-w)`. Conversely, every rational section of `L^(-w)` evaluates on the fibre to a weight-`w` homogeneous rational function.

Thus a field-valued readout of nonzero fibre weight is not additional structure generated by the torsor. It is precisely a rational trivialization choice.

## 3. No canonical regular weight-one coordinate

If `L` is nontrivial in `Pic^0(X)`, it has no everywhere regular nowhere-vanishing section. Therefore `L^x` has no global regular weight-one scalar coordinate.

A rational coordinate exists, but its zeros and poles record a divisor representative of the line-bundle class. In the C13 normalization, selecting the representative encoded by `D` is exactly the gauge computation represented by `h_G`.

This is an exact structural statement. It is not an arithmetic-circuit lower bound for computing one prescribed rational trivialization.

## 4. Gauge transformation law for endpoint ratios

Let `R` be one rational weight-one readout and let `u in K(X)^x`. Every other readout in the same generic fibre coordinate class has the form

```text
R'(v)=u(pi(v))*R(v),                              (C14.6)
```

where `pi:T->X` is the projection.

For two base points related by public translation, the scalar endpoint comparison transforms as

```text
boxed:
Z_R'(P,G)
 =Z_R(P,G)*u(P)/u(P+G).                           (C14.7)
```

Therefore the endpoint scalar is not an invariant of the abstract torsor or of the compact line-bundle class alone. It depends on the selected meromorphic gauge.

Weight-zero rational invariants avoid this ambiguity, but they descend to base functions and no longer read fibre orientation. They may still encode parity through a high-degree base formula, but then the generalized-Jacobian lift has provided no separate compression.
