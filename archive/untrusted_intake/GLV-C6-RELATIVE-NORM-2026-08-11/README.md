## Correction to the original theta direction

The coordinate

\[
q(P)=y(P)^2=x(P)^3+b
\]

on

\[
E_b:y^2=x^3+b
\]

is invariant under the full group generated by

\[
\phi(x,y)=(\beta x,y),\qquad -1:(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y).
\]

However,

\[
q-b=x^3.
\]

Thus q is only a translation of the already studied invariant `u=x^3`. The potentially different mechanism is not a new coordinate. It is a direct relative-phase norm that eliminates the GLV phase variables before polynomial solving.

## y-coordinate local relation

Set

\[
e_1=y_1+y_2+y_3,\quad
 e_2=y_1y_2+y_1y_3+y_2y_3,\quad
 e_3=y_1y_2y_3.
\]

Define

\[
T_{3,b}=e_2(e_2+9b)^2-27(e_3+b e_1)^2.
\]

For three intersections with a line `y=mx+c`, coefficient comparison gives

\[
e_1=m^3+3c,\quad e_2=3c^2,\quad e_3=c^3-bm^3,
\]

so `T3=0`.

On the regular branch

\[
D=e_2+9b\ne0,
\]

one recovers

\[
c=3(e_3+b e_1)/D,\quad m^3=e_1-3c,\quad x_i=(y_i-c)/m.
\]

On the exceptional branch `D=0`, one uses

\[
c^2=-3b,\quad m^3=e_1-3c.
\]

The toy checker recovered all 760 tested exceptional zeros.

## Full C6 orbit polynomial

Define

\[
U_{3,b}(q_1,q_2,q_3)
=
\prod_{\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\in\{\pm1\}}
T_{3,b}(y_1,\epsilon_2y_2,\epsilon_3y_3),
\quad q_i=y_i^2.
\]

Exact properties:

- multidegree `(6,6,6)` in `(q1,q2,q3)`;
- total degree `12`;
- `183` expanded terms;
- leading coefficient in `q3` equal to `(q1-q2)^6`.

## Central relative-phase norm identity

In the ring with `beta^2+beta+1=0`, sparse integer arithmetic verifies

\[
\boxed{
U_{3,b}(x_1^3+b,x_2^3+b,x_3^3+b)
=
\prod_{a,c\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z}
S_{3,b}(x_1,\beta^a x_2,\beta^c x_3).
}
\]

The nine factors represent

\[
C_3^3/\Delta C_3\cong C_3^2.
\]

Both sparse sides have SHA-256

```text
4c9975adce8af4f2ef56743a2df2a9b42e20299caa44ce0299cf55c9e142c0f6
```

This differs from the naive independent cube quotient. The naive presentation forgets the relative phases and retains explicit `x_i` lift variables. The norm polynomial existentially combines all relative phases into one equation and permits local orbit-tag recovery afterward.

## Orbit factorization

For points in distinct free C6 orbits,

\[
U_{3,b}(q(P),q(Q),Z)
=
(q(P)-q(Q))^6
\prod_{h\in C_6}(Z-q(P+hQ)).
\]

This was checked on 1,760 toy point pairs with zero failures.

## Phase-tag recovery and collisions

For the scalar eigenvalue `lambda`, local coefficients lie in

\[
\{\pm1,\pm\lambda,\pm\lambda^2\}.
\]

All 87 coordinate relations in nine tested prime-order toy configurations were constructively converted to group relations.

A local phase collision satisfies, for `Q=[r]P`,

\[
r=(g-1)/(h_1-gh_2),\qquad g,h_1,h_2\in C_6.
\]

Exact enumeration in the secp256k1 scalar ring gives 42 nonzero ratios, of which 6 are same-orbit cases. Only 36 ratios remain for distinct orbits. For a uniformly random scalar ratio outside the orbit, the corresponding exact upper fraction is `36/(n-7)`, below `2^-250.83`. Factor-base uniformity is not proved.

## Solver signal

The direct orbit polynomial was compared with the faithful system containing explicit `x_i`, `u_i-x_i^3=0`, and factor-base equations in `u_i`.

On tiny SymPy grevlex instances:

- at one C6 orbit, direct q was about 1.55x to 2.01x faster;
- at two C6 orbits, faithful u timed out at 10 to 12 seconds while direct q completed in 0.145 to 0.259 seconds;
- randomized direct-q runs grew from roughly 0.09 to 0.25 seconds at `k=2` to roughly 3.9 to 5.8 seconds at `k=3`;
- the first randomized `p=823,k=4` instance timed out at 20 seconds.

The sharp growth is negative evidence against an immediate asymptotic improvement. The remaining falsifiable question is whether optimized F4 matrices are consistently smaller than the faithful baseline for bounded toy sizes.
